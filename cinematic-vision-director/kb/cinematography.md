# Cinematography Grammar

The system's internalized film language. Every term is paired with its **psychological or narrative function** — because a cinematographer does not choose a tool for what it is, but for what it *does to the viewer*. This is decision logic, not a glossary. When the brain (Layer 3) selects craft, it reasons from these functions.

This file also serves as the **color-grade diagnostic key** for Layer 1: read the existing grade in the uploaded frame against the Color Grade section to infer mood.

---

## Shot Types — Distance Is Meaning

How much you show controls how the viewer feels about the subject.

- **Wide shot** — the full scene; context, the subject's place in the world. Smallness, belonging, or exposure.
- **Establishing shot** — introduces location and atmosphere; orients before intimacy.
- **Extreme wide / long shot** — the subject swallowed by environment. Isolation, insignificance, the sublime.
- **Medium shot** — waist up; conversational, neutral, the everyday register of human exchange.
- **Close-up** — the face or a detail; intimacy, emotion, we are *with* them.
- **Extreme close-up** — a single feature: eyes, hands, an object. Heightened tension, fixation, the detail that matters too much.
- **Over-the-shoulder** — perspective and relationship; we share someone's vantage in a charged exchange.
- **POV** — through the subject's eyes; total identification, we *become* them.
- **Two-shot** — two figures framed together; relationship, the space (or tension) between them.
- **High angle** — looking down; the subject diminished, vulnerable, observed.
- **Low angle** — looking up; the subject empowered, dominant, looming.
- **Dutch angle** — tilted horizon; unease, instability, something wrong.
- **Aerial / bird's-eye** — from far above; fate, detachment, the pattern only we can see.

## Lens — Psychological Distance

The focal length sets the viewer's relationship to the subject. This is the most under-used instinct in machine footage: lens is *emotion*, not field of view.

- **Wide-angle** — expands space and depth; near a face it distorts and looms (unease, power, the grotesque). At distance it isolates a figure in vastness.
- **Ultra-wide** — sweeping, dramatic, slightly unreal; scale and spectacle.
- **Telephoto** — compresses distance, flattens layers, isolates subject from background. Voyeurism, surveillance, the watched. Hope or escape compressed flat.
- **Prime** — sharp, fast, rich; clean and intentional, the "filmic" baseline.
- **Anamorphic** — widescreen, oval bokeh, horizontal flares; epic, cinematic, the big canvas.
- **Macro** — extreme detail; the world too close, texture as obsession.
- **Fish-eye** — distorted bubble; psychedelia, derangement, a broken POV.
- **Super 35** — balanced cinematic depth; versatile film-standard feel.
- **Tilt-shift** — selective plane of focus; miniaturizes, makes the real toylike.
- **Soft focus** — gentle dreamlike blur; memory, romance, the idealized.
- **Portrait lens** — flattering bokeh on close subjects; tenderness, attention.
- **Vintage lens** — vignetting, softness, character flaws; nostalgia, the analog past.
- **High-speed lens** — clean in low light; night, available darkness, naturalism after dusk.

## Camera Motion — The Move Is the Emotion

Every move must obey **Motivated Motion**: it moves *because* something in the scene causes it. The function below is the *why* you name.

- **Push-in (dolly in)** — slow move toward subject; rising interiority, realization, intensifying focus, the thought arriving.
- **Pull-out (dolly out)** — withdrawing; isolation, abandonment, the reveal of a context that recontextualizes everything.
- **Tracking** — follows the subject's movement; accompaniment, we travel *with* them.
- **Lateral track / trucking** — sideways glide; observation, the passing world, parallel lives.
- **Pan** — horizontal sweep from a fixed point; surveying, following a glance, connecting two things.
- **Tilt** — vertical sweep; revealing scale up or weight down, the look raised or lowered.
- **Crane / pedestal** — camera lifts or lowers bodily; revelation, scale, release, or descent into.
- **Arc** — circling the subject; reconsideration, examination, the subject turned over in the mind, instability orbiting stillness.
- **Handheld** — unsteady, organic; immediacy, unease, the documentary real, nerves.
- **Steadicam** — fluid motion while moving; immersive, dreamlike continuity, gliding through.
- **Roll** — rotating the frame; vertigo, derangement, the world coming loose.
- **Rack focus** — shifting focus between planes; redirecting attention, the realization that what mattered was elsewhere.
- **Vertigo (dolly zoom)** — zoom against dolly; dread, dissociation, the ground falling away beneath a fixed gaze.
- **Whip pan** — fast blur pan; energy, a hard cut within a take, time or place snapping forward.
- **Zoom** — optical magnification (no parallax); flatter, more clinical or retro than a dolly; the eye narrowing.

## Camera Speed — Time as a Tool

- **Slow motion** — stretches a moment; weight, beauty, the unbearable significance of an instant.
- **Fast motion** — compresses; urgency, comedy, the mechanical churn.
- **Time-lapse** — long duration condensed; indifference of time, the world moving past stillness.
- **Hyperlapse** — time-lapse with motion; propulsion through space and time.
- **Speed ramp** — a shift within the shot; emphasis, the snap from fast to slow at the decisive beat.
- **Looping** — rhythmic repetition; the hypnotic, the trapped, the cyclical.
- **Strobe** — staccato bursts; panic, the fractured, the edge of perception.
- **Natural dynamic** — smooth continuous real-time motion; the honest baseline.

## Lighting — Direction Is Grammar

Where the light *comes from* carries the meaning. Read the existing direction in the frame (Layer 1); any choice must honor it.

- **Fill light** — softens shadow, balances the key; reduces drama, evens the read.
- **Backlight** — from behind; separation, mystery, the sacred, the silhouette's edge of light.
- **Side light** — rakes across; texture, division, the moral edge, half in dark.
- **Hard light** — sharp shadows, high contrast; harshness, clarity, exposure, threat.
- **Soft light** — diffused; flattery, tenderness, the gentle.
- **Practical lighting** — visible in-scene sources (lamps, screens, neon); motivated naturalism, the world lighting itself.
- **Ambient light** — general, natural fill; the unremarkable, the documentary.
- **High-key** — bright, low-contrast, airy; optimism, safety, the clean and commercial.
- **Low-key** — dark, high-contrast; mystery, dread, noir, the moody.
- **Color gel** — colored light; mood injected, the heightened and unreal.
- **Motivated lighting** — mimics a real source's logic; believable, grounded.
- **Rembrandt** — triangle of light on the shadowed cheek; classical portrait depth, dignity.
- **Silhouette** — subject dark against lit background; anonymity, mystery, the iconic shape.
- **Underlight (from below)** — the unnatural, threat, horror, the campfire tale.

## Color Grade — The Tonal Spine (Layer 1 Diagnostic Key)

Read the grade already in the frame; map it to mood; carry the mood as constraint. The inferred story and the camera move must *agree* with it.

- **Warm tone** — golden, amber; nostalgia, intimacy, comfort, memory.
- **Cool tone** — blue, green; detachment, calm, loneliness, the clinical.
- **High contrast** — deep shadows, bright highlights; drama, tension, the stark.
- **Low contrast** — lifted, muted; softness, melancholy, the dreamlike, the faded.
- **Vintage look** — faded color and grain; retro, the remembered, the analog.
- **Teal & orange** — complementary skin/shadow split; commercial, blockbuster, heightened.
- **Monochrome (B&W)** — timelessness, the essential, emotional abstraction.
- **Desaturated** — drained color; bleakness, grit, exhaustion, the severe.
- **Sepia** — warm brown; the past, the antique, faded memory.
- **Bleach bypass** — crushed, metallic, harsh; war, grit, the unforgiving.
- **Day-for-night** — cool darkened daylight; unease, the false night.
- **Pastel palette** — soft muted hues; whimsy, tenderness, the gentle unreal.
- **High saturation** — vivid, popping; energy, the heightened, the pop-bright.

## Composition — Leading the Eye

The eye goes first to the brightest, sharpest, most saturated point. Compose and move to control its path.

- **Center framing** — strong, direct focus; confrontation, stillness, formality.
- **Leading lines** — guide the eye toward the subject; direction, inevitability.
- **Symmetry** — mirrored balance; order, control, the unsettlingly perfect.
- **Depth** — fore/mid/background layering; the three-dimensional, the runway for motion.
- **Framing (frame-within-frame)** — objects enclose the subject; entrapment, focus, the watched.
- **Negative space** — emptiness around the subject; isolation, room for the unseen to arrive, anticipation.
- **Asymmetry** — off-balance; dynamism, tension, the unresolved.
- **Foreground interest** — objects near camera; depth, the voyeur's vantage, something to push past.
- **Diagonal composition** — angled energy; movement, instability, drive.
- **Texture** — tactile surface emphasis; the material, the real.
- **Repetition** — repeated elements; rhythm, the pattern, the institutional.
- **Contrast** — difference in light/color/texture; the eye's anchor, the point of attention.

## Balance — Visual Weight

- **Color balance** — complementary or contrasting hues distributed across the frame.
- **Light/dark balance** — distribution of shadow and highlight.
- **Tonal balance** — warm against cool.
- **Contrast balance** — harmonizing opposed elements.
- **Radial balance** — elements radiating from a center.
- **Proportional balance** — element size matched to importance.
- **Textural balance** — varied surfaces in tension.
- **Pattern balance** — repetition for rhythm.

## How Layer 3 Uses This

1. Take the **beat** (Layer 2) and the **grade-mood** (Layer 1).
2. Pick the **shot distance** that matches the emotional proximity you want.
3. Pick the **lens** for psychological distance — not field of view.
4. Pick **one camera move** whose function matches the beat, and name its **motivation** (Motivated Motion).
5. Confirm **mood–camera agreement** — the move speaks the grade's language.
6. Set **pacing** — the hold before the move; allocate the beats in seconds.
7. Apply **Restraint** — one move, two to three cues, cut the rest.
